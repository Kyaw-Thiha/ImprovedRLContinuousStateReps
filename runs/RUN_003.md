## 2. What was run (protocol)

Grid: 3 representations (discrete, SSP, tile_coding) x 3 centering modes (none, simple, value) x 2 architectures
(linear readout, deep MLP) = 18 cells, DQN, CartPole-v1 (max 500 steps), 1000 training episodes per run.

1. **Search (RUN_002, finished earlier).** 500 Optuna trials per cell (TPE + MedianPruner, 1 seed per trial).
   Objective: the last greedy checkpoint. Greedy = argmax over Q, no epsilon. 15 checkpoints per run (every 66
   episodes, 10 episodes each).
2. **Filter (this run).** The top 20 completed trials by the mean of their last 5 greedy checkpoints are each re-run on
   10 NEW seeds (seed offset 1000). A candidate's score = mean over seeds of (per-seed mean of its last 5 greedy
   checkpoints). Winner = best mean; ties go to the lower `np.std`. It is a flat 20 x 10 filter with no staged cut,
   because the pilot (handoff 02) showed seed noise (sd about 90) is about 3x the config-to-config spread (sd about 30).
3. **Final eval (this run).** The winner is run on 20 more fresh seeds (offset 2000), same score. **The numbers in this
   file are from this step, not from the filter.** The filter mean is reported only to show the winner's-curse gap.
4. **Reported statistics.** Mean +/- `np.std` across the 20 seeds (population std), "solved" = share of seeds whose
   score >= 475, bootstrap 95% CI of the mean (5,000 resamples, computed for this file).
5. **Fixed DQN settings (never tuned):** replay buffer 5,000, batch 64, target update every 10 gradient updates,
   learning_starts 1,000, train_freq 4, gamma 0.99, Adam, grad-clip 10. `sample_dqn_params` exists in
   `scripts/run_hparam_search_dqn.py` but the objective never calls it. Searched per cell: lr, eps, plus the
   representation knobs (SSP `length_scale` and `n_rotates` 4-20 with `n_scales`=5; tile `iht_size`
   {32768, 65536, 131072}, `num_tilings` {16, 32}, `tiles_per_dim` {10, 12, 16}; discrete `n_bins`), plus the
   centering parameter for `simple` (beta, range 1e-5..1e-1) and `value` (eta, range 1e-4..1).
6. **Pipeline.** One CPU pod per cell, each chaining search top-up -> filter -> final eval -> push to R2 -> self-delete,
   4 runs in parallel per pod. Code and flags: `scripts/run_hparam_search_dqn.py` (`--workers`, `--final-eval`,
   `--idle-after`, `--self-delete`), `scripts/parallel_jobs.py`, `scripts/launch_cell_pod.py`,
   `scripts/watch_cells.py`. Design notes are in handoff 02 and decision-log entries 14-18.

Caveats on the protocol that affect how to read the numbers:
- Environment resets are unseeded, so re-running a seed does NOT reproduce the same numbers. Treat a re-run as a new
  sample.
- Initial network weights follow the run seed in the filter and final eval, but the search used the legacy fixed init.
- Wall times in section 4 (table "cost") were measured with 4 runs sharing one pod and different winner configs, on
  different hosts. They are NOT clean speed comparisons between representations.
- "Simple" centering is applied naively to an episodic task (see conclusion 6). It is reported for completeness; it
  was NOT designed to work on CartPole.

- discrete/simple: deep - linear = +4.3
- discrete/value: deep - linear = -45.0

Deep networks do not systematically help: tile coding is flat or slightly worse with a deep head (-9, -17, +3), discrete
gets worse or flat (-28, +4, -45; all of them are bad anyway), and SSP improves (+6 to +52), most when centering is
'simple' (which is the worst case for SSP in the first place). The deep SSP network is learning a nonlinear readout on
a 951-dimensional feature vector and gets 486.4 on `none` vs 469.0 for the linear one.

### 4.8 Metric definitions

- **score (primary)** = a seed's mean over its last 5 greedy checkpoints (10 episodes each, argmax, no epsilon).
- **solved** = share of seeds with score >= 475 (CartPole's usual solved level is an average of 475 over 100 episodes
  on v1; here it is applied to the last-5 checkpoint mean as a seed-level pass/fail).
- **bootstrap CI** = percentile interval of the mean after resampling the 20 seed scores with replacement 5,000 times.
- **filter mean** = the winner's mean score over the 10 filter seeds (offset 1000).
- **last1** = score of the final checkpoint only. **final-policy eval** = a separate 100-episode greedy eval of the
  final network. **online return** = mean training return over the last 100 training episodes (includes epsilon).
- **stability metrics (4.3)** = from `eval_history` (15 values per seed).

## 5. Conclusions

Confidence is noted. "DQN setup" = the fixed settings in section 2. Everything is CartPole-only.

1. **Tile coding >= SSP on score under DQN on CartPole** (high confidence for the pairs that exist).
   `none/deep` is a tie (489.7 vs 486.4; diff CI spans 0). `none/linear` and `value/linear` favour tile coding by 27-30
   points with CIs that exclude zero. `simple` favours tile coding by 116-185. `value/deep` (the last cell) also favours tile coding: 498.0 vs 474.6, diff +23 with CI [+12, +35].
   Overall tile coding wins 5 of the 6 pairs and ties 1.
   SSP is a solid representation here (80% of seeds solved for `ssp/none/deep`, 55-60% for the linear ones) but it is
   not better than tile coding. Discrete is far worse than both (221-266, 0-5% solved), and a deep network does not
   rescue it.
2. **"SSP is 30-65x smaller" is NOT demonstrated.** The winners do differ by 30-65x in parameters, but tile coding's
   hash-table floor (32,768) sat above anything SSP was allowed to be (201-1,001 dims), so the ranges never overlapped.
   At equal parameter count tile coding already matches or beats SSP: `tile_coding/none/linear` has 65,538 params and
   498.6 vs `ssp/none/deep` 65,218 params and 486.4. A size sweep that lowers the tile floors and extends SSP's
   `n_rotates` down to 1 is required before making any size claim, in either direction.
3. **SSP dimension has a threshold effect, not "no effect"** (medium confidence; confounded with other
   hyperparameters and TPE's sampling). Spearman(dim, score) over completed search trials is weak (|rho| <= 0.11) in five
   of six SSP cells, but `ssp/value/linear` has rho = +0.34 (0% of trials below 500 dims reach 450; 46-59% of those
   above). Below about 500 dims the median score is much worse in `ssp/none/linear` (19 vs 422-433) and
   `ssp/none/deep` (257 vs 377-459). Yet the `ssp/value/deep` winner has only 201 dims and scores 474.6. The old MiniGrid
   finding (64-532 dims, little effect) does not transfer cleanly. Dimensions below 201 were never tested.
4. **SSP is harder to tune than tile coding, easier than discrete.** Share of the first 50 search trials with a last-5
   greedy mean >= 450: tile none/value 38-46%, tile simple/linear 28%, SSP none/value 16-22%, SSP simple 0-2%, discrete
   0-2%.
5. **SSP is less stable than tile coding under DQN** (high confidence in the observation, hypothesis for the cause).
   Among trials that ever reached >= 475, the share ending with a last-5 mean < 400 ("learn-then-collapse") is 3-12%
   for tile linear, 18-34% for SSP none/value, about 90% for SSP simple. At final eval, tile linear stays above 475 in
   19-20 of 20 seeds, SSP none/value in 14-18. **Hypothesis, not tested here:** SSP features are dense and global, so a
   gradient step on states visited when the policy is good also moves Q-values of failure states (interference), and the
   fixed DQN settings (a replay buffer of 5,000 holds about 10 successful episodes; target update every 10 updates)
   make it worse. Tile features are sparse and local, which avoids this. Analogous literature (neural features, not SSP):
   Liu et al. 2019 and 2023. The direct test is the DQN-settings test in handoff 03, section 4.4.
6. **Centering.** The implementation matches Naik et al. 2024 (eq. 4 for `simple`, Algorithm 3 for `value`); no bug was
   found. The paper itself says centering is not applicable to episodic tasks. CartPole pays +1 per step, so `simple`
   centering drives the centered reward toward zero and erases the failure signal. This was shown dose-dependently on
   a good config in the pilot (ssp/none/deep config #154: 497 without centering; with `simple` centering 469 at
   beta 1e-5, 338 at 1e-4, 271 at 1e-2). All six `simple` winners pin beta near its lower bound. `value` centering is
   harmless (SSP 469-475, tile 495). So the `simple` rows are "centering applied naively to an episodic task", which is
   how they are labelled. Whether `simple` is also harmful under A2C is open: the legacy A2C results (not comparable)
   showed `simple` as benign there (SSP 488.6, tile 490.9, discrete 378 > none 118), and the explanation is a hypothesis
   (in A2C the policy is a separate parameter set that stops changing when the centered TD error goes to zero; in DQN the
   greedy policy is the argmax of values that decay toward zero). Decision-log entry 17 ("centering hurts on episodic
   CartPole") should be read as DQN-specific until A2C is re-evaluated.
7. **The A2C-vs-DQN difference for SSP is algorithm and protocol, not a neuron layer.** Legacy A2C had SSP >= tile coding
   in all three centerings, with a plain linear readout (`state_neurons: null`; no Nengo neuron layer) on both. What
   differs: on-policy TD(0) with plain SGD (lr 0.4-0.8), no replay, no target network, and an ONLINE metric instead
   of greedy evaluation.
8. **Seed-to-seed reliability is NOT a place SSP shines here** (high confidence). Tile std is 3.6-7 (linear) and 2.8-20
   (deep); SSP std is 21-35 (up to 102 for `simple`). The user's paper reports 150x lower terminal variance for SSP than
   for a deep A2C MLP; that comparison is against a deep MLP, not against tile coding, and our data does not extend it.
9. **Deep networks do not help** discrete or tile coding on this task (section 4.7; tile deep minus linear is -17 to +3).
10. **The primary metric is not an artifact of the 10-episode checkpoints.** The separate 100-episode final-policy eval
    agrees with the last-5 mean within noise for every strong cell (section 4.2).

Retractions made during this work (so nobody re-derives them):
- An early hypothesis that a fixed random neuron layer explained why SSP does worse under DQN than under A2C was wrong for
  the grid's A2C numbers (no neuron layer was used there).
- "SSP dimension has no huge effect" (imported from the MiniGrid repo) is only partly supported (conclusion 3).
- The size advantage (conclusion 2) looked like a result and is a search-range artifact.
- An early estimate of "a few hours" for a multi-objective search was too low for the code as built (one pod runs trials
  serially: 5-24 hours per 500-trial search).

## 6. Things worth knowing that the tables do not say

- **Winner's curse is real and family-dependent.** Search rank did not predict fresh-seed performance (pilot: Spearman
  0.05 in `ssp/simple/deep`, about 90% of score variance was seed noise, ICC 0.10), and 3-seed cuts were close to
  coin flips among top candidates. This is why the filter is flat 20 x 10 and why only final-eval numbers are quoted.
- **Time to first solved vs time to stay solved** are different questions (section 4.3). SSP reaches 475 about as early as
  tile coding, then loses it more often.
- **Reproducibility.** Environment resets are unseeded. A second run of the same winner is a fresh sample of the same
  distribution; the pending policy-saving retrain (below) doubles as a free replicate.
- **What was NOT measured** (so a reader does not assume it): robustness to changed dynamics or noisy observations,
  deployment size and latency, behaviour outside CartPole, sensitivity to the fixed DQN settings, any A2C result under the
  greedy protocol, and any raw-input baseline (the user explicitly declined that one).
- **Known imperfections:** the CSV tree for `discrete/none/linear` is incomplete on R2 (JSONL and summary intact);
  pod self-delete was never confirmed to work and did not work for the last cell (the watcher deleted it; all 18 pods are gone);
  CI and stability numbers above were computed after the fact from the summary files, not stored by the run.

## 7. Discussion: where SSP could be genuinely good, and what to run (summary of the design conversation)

The user's goal is to find where SSP representations are actually better, not to re-confirm them on CartPole. CartPole
under DQN says: competitive, not best. The long-form version of this section, with costs, is in
`docs/handoffs/03-prelim-results-and-eval-plan.md` (sections 4-6); this is the short form.

**SSP strengths from the literature, and what our data says about each**
1. *Smooth, tunable kernel* (the dot product of two SSPs is a shift-invariant kernel with a length-scale; no bin edges).
   Our data: far better than discrete, no better than tile coding on CartPole. The robustness suite tests this directly.
2. *Size independent of resolution and linear in input dimension* (dim = 2*(n_rotates*n_scales)*(d+1)+1, vs tile grids
   that grow like tiles^d unless hashed, and the full Fourier basis that grows like (n+1)^d). NOT tested beyond d = 4.
   Caveat (Chouinard 2026, arXiv 2605.08458): the default hypergeometric kernel flattens as d grows at a fixed
   length-scale, so the length-scale must be re-tuned per dimension; the Gaussian-kernel variant does not flatten.
3. *Compact footprint.* True for our winners but not yet size-matched (conclusion 2).
4. *Compositional algebra* (binding multiple variables/objects into one fixed-size vector). Demonstrated for Bayesian
   optimization (Furlong et al. 2026), not found in RL; cannot be tested on CartPole.
5. *Spatial/metric variables* (grid/place-cell structure), the natural fit for positions and displacements, e.g. the
   end-effector-to-cube and cube-to-goal terms planned for manipulation.
6. *The user's paper claims* (ICCM 2022): low seed variance vs a deep A2C MLP, and robustness when RatBox speed was
   halved while the tabular baseline failed. The paper compared HexSSP only to tabular discretization and a deep MLP,
   not tile coding. Variance: not supported against tile coding here. Robustness: the one claim that is both open and
   cheap to test.
7. *Neuromorphic compatibility* (Furlong et al. report 60-200x less compute and 30-188x less energy per sample on
   neuromorphic hardware, for Bayesian optimization). We cannot measure this here.

**Planned evaluation additions (not yet run; user's constraint: every metric must answer a question that could change a
decision)**
- *Free analyses* on the existing data first (most are already in section 4: stability, CIs, pair comparisons).
- *One retraining pass that SAVES policies* (network weights, the tile-coding hash table, the SSP encoding matrix, the
  exact params and seed), verified by reloading and re-evaluating a few seeds; plus per-episode returns for the
  100-episode final eval, and for centering cells a log of R-bar and mean max-Q at each checkpoint (a direct test of the
  `simple`-centering explanation). Push to a NEW prefix, e.g. `stage0_cartpole/eval_v3/<study>/policies/`.
- *Robustness suite* (test-time only, no retraining; mirrors the RatBox protocol): pole length x0.5/1.5/2, pole and cart
  mass x0.5/2, gravity x0.8/1.2, force magnitude x0.7/1.3, observation noise at 1/5/10% of per-dimension state std,
  action delay of 1-2 steps, 5% random actions, widened initial states. Pre-declared interpretation: SSP's robustness
  counts only if it beats TILE CODING's retained score by more than bootstrap noise; beating discrete is expected and
  is not evidence. If tile coding retains as well, the robustness claim is generic to fixed features, which is itself
  a reportable result.
- *Deployment metrics:* model bytes including encoder constants, int8/fp16 retention, CPU latency per action (SSP
  encode is a matmul + complex exponential + inverse FFT; tile coding is 16-32 lookups). SSP should win memory and may
  lose compute; we have never measured it.
- *DQN-settings test* (replay buffer 5,000 vs 50,000; target update 10 vs 100 vs 500; 10 seeds): is the SSP collapse
  an artifact of the fixed settings?
- *A2C re-evaluation via a PyTorch port* (the old code is Nengo on Python 3.9; pods have no Nengo). No new search if the
  port reproduces the old online scores within noise on 10 seeds (gate: ssp/none about 498, tile/none about 495,
  discrete/none about 118); otherwise retune only lr and eps (about 30 trials). Then evaluate with the same greedy suite.

**Experiments to show where SSP is actually good (priority order)**
1. Robustness suite (above), DQN and A2C.
2. *Size sweep:* take the `none/linear` winners, vary only the size knobs (tile `iht_size` far below 32,768, smaller
   `tiles_per_dim`; SSP `n_rotates` 1-20), 10 seeds per size, a light retune only if small sizes look penalized.
   Success = SSP keeps its score where tile coding degrades. Tile coding matching SSP down to a few thousand params is
   also a valid result.
3. *Dimension scaling (the user considers it very important):* CartPole with a stacked history of the last k states
   (k = 2, 4, 8 -> 8, 16, 32 dims; extra dims are real but redundant), then Acrobot (6 dims) and LunarLander (8).
   Comparators: SSP vs tile coding (hashing and tilings over low-order subsets of dimensions, the standard
   high-dimension practice) with discrete as a reference. Re-tune the SSP length-scale per dimension; a Gaussian-kernel
   SSP is a one-off diagnostic at the largest d if SSP struggles, not a baseline. A Fourier-basis baseline (Konidaris et
   al. 2011) is a second wave. Small searches (40-60 trials per task per representation), not 500.
4. Centering cleanup: a continuing CartPole variant, a wider beta range (about 1e-8..1e-1), and the A2C-vs-DQN
   explanation.
5. Later: hex-vs-random SSP and random-Fourier-feature ablations, a rotation-robustness test, a compositional multi-object
   task (the only place binding can show; needs a custom environment).
6. mjlab (robotics stage), after the Stage-0 questions are answered: verified facts are MuJoCo Warp (GPU), thousands of
   parallel environments, NVIDIA GPU required for training; the user's laptop has an RTX 3050 Laptop GPU (4 GB), so
   expect small environment counts, and RunPod CPU pods cannot run it. Its observation and event managers provide noise,
   delay, domain randomization and pushes, so robustness tests are nearly free there.

**Do NOT run:** a raw-input baseline (declined by the user), seed-reliability as a separate experiment, any further
500-trial search, a full multi-objective size search (a sweep is cheaper and answers the same question), mjlab before the
Stage-0 questions above.

**Literature touched** (alphaXiv and web; not exhaustive): the user's ICCM 2022 paper; Komer & Eliasmith 2020 (SSP beat
six other encodings for supervised navigation); Konidaris et al. 2011 (Fourier basis); Chouinard 2026 (kernel flattening
with dimension); Furlong et al. 2026 (SSP-based Bayesian optimization, neuromorphic); Krausse et al. 2025; Mir et al.
2024 (hexagonal vs square grids performed the same in a memory-recall model); Damberger et al. 2025 (SSP place cells for
adaptive control of a 3-link arm); Liu et al. 2019 and 2023 (interference and sparse features); Naik et al. 2024
(reward centering). No paper was found comparing SSP against tile coding at matched size in RL, or SSP dimension scaling
in RL.
