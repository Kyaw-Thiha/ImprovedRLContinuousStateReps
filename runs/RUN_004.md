## 1. Protocol

Decisions made with the user are marked (user).

**What is run**

1. **Policy pass** (`scripts/run_policy_pass.py`, per-seed worker `scripts/policy_pass_worker.py`). Retrain each cell's
   RUN_003 winner config (read from `stage0_cartpole/best_params/dqn_<rep>_<cen>[_deep]_v2.yaml`, read only) on 20 NEW
   seeds, 3000-3019, with the env seeded (`seed_env=True`), and save the FINAL policy of every seed (user). No peak or
   intermediate checkpoint is saved (user). A policy is done only when the trial's in-memory verify replay (extra greedy
   episodes before saving) matches a replay from the reloaded file exactly (`max_abs_diff` 0.0); the worker re-verifies
   from the saved file and only then uploads. Resumable: a seed is done only when its record is error-free.
2. **Probe states** (`scripts/make_probe_states.py`, `_shared/probe_states.npz`): 256 fixed states (128 uniform in
   |x|<2, |v|<2, |theta|<0.2, |omega|<2; 128 from a scripted balancing controller) for training-time Q diagnostics, plus
   the pooled observation-noise scale `noise_sigma`. Generated deterministically, independent of any policy.
3. **Robustness** (`rl/perturbations.py`, `scripts/run_robustness.py`, `scripts/analyze_robustness.py`). Saved policies,
   greedy, 21 conditions in 10 families, 20 episodes per (seed, condition), no retraining. Run on all 18 cells.
4. **Deployment** (`scripts/run_deployment.py`): size at fp32/fp16/int8; greedy return after simulated fp16/int8 weight
   quantization (and an fp16 SSP phase matrix), paired with the fp32 policy; per-call latency of encode / q / act plus
   analytic operation counts. Latency is only run on a quiet pod (`--sections latency`).
5. **DQN-settings test** (`scripts/run_policy_pass.py --variant-buffer-size / --variant-target-update-freq`): replay
   buffer 5000 vs 50000 and target update 10 vs 100 vs 500, on cells {tile_coding, ssp} x none x {linear, deep}, 10
   seeds 3000-3009 paired with the baseline pass (same initial weights and env seed stream), no policies saved (user).
   Runs right after the baseline pass is verified and BEFORE any size sweep (user).

**Seeds.** Policy pass 3000-3019 (20 per cell; user). Variants 3000-3009. Earlier phases: search 0-499, filter 1000+,
final eval 2000+, so 3000+ is an independent replicate of the RUN_003 final eval (user). Within a run with `seed_env`:
first training reset seeded with the run seed, first greedy-checkpoint evaluation with seed+1,000,000, verify replay with
seed+2,000,000; quantization rollouts use seed+3,000,000. Robustness episode seeds depend only on (condition, episode
index), so every policy of every cell sees the same initial states and noise (common random numbers).

**Fixed settings.** Same training budget and metrics as RUN_003 (1000 episodes, 15 greedy checkpoints x 10 episodes,
score = mean of the last 5 checkpoints, final-policy 100-episode greedy eval, solved = >= 475). Fixed DQN settings unless
a variant overrides them: replay buffer 5000, batch 64, target update every 10 updates, learning_starts 1000, train_freq
4, gamma 0.99, Adam, grad-clip 10. lr, eps and representation knobs come from each cell's winner and are NOT retuned for
any variant. Nothing in `refilter_v2/`, `best_params/`, `optuna_dbs/` is written by eval_v3 code.

**Robustness conditions** (families; `rl.perturbations.FAMILIES`):
baseline; pole_length x0.5/1.5/2/4; pole_mass x0.5/2; cart_mass x0.5/2; gravity x0.8/1.2; force x0.7/1.3;
obs_noise 0.01/0.05/0.1; action_delay 1/2; random_action 0.05; wide_init 0.1/0.2.
Semantics:
- `obs_noise_f`: N(0, (f*noise_sigma)^2) added to what the agent sees (including the reset observation); true state,
  termination and reward untouched. `noise_sigma` is policy-independent, pooled over 50 seeded episodes of a scripted PD
  controller with 10% random actions: [0.4341, 0.3061, 0.0272, 0.2106]. With it the three levels are MILD on the pole
  angle (0.1 -> about 0.0027 rad), so they may saturate at 500 for every policy. Plan: look at the pilot cell first, then
  decide whether to add 0.25 and 0.5.
- `random_action_p`: with probability p the applied action is replaced by a uniform draw (may equal the decision), so
  with 2 actions the wrong-action rate is p/2. Applied after the delay.
- `action_delay_d`: the action applied at step t was decided at step t-d; the first d applied actions are the policy's
  decision on the reset observation (CartPole has no no-op).
- Physics conditions scale attributes of `env.unwrapped` and recompute `total_mass` / `polemass_length`; `length` is the
  half pole length. `wide_init_b`: initial state uniform in [-b, b]^4.
- Each episode runs on `policy.clone()`, so tile-coding IHT growth never leaks between episodes or conditions.

**What each test answers, and what would change which decision**

- Policy pass replicate vs RUN_003. Q: are the RUN_003 cell means reproducible on fresh, env-seeded seeds? If a cell's
  mean differs from RUN_003 by more than bootstrap noise (look at the winner's-curse cells: ssp/value, discrete), report
  the replicate as the headline for that cell and treat RUN_003 as optimistic; if all agree, RUN_003's ranking stands.
- Stability and Q diagnostics (probe max-Q and action gap over training, by centering). Q: does the collapse coincide
  with Q-value drift or a vanishing action gap (e.g. value centering or SSP)? A systematic divergence in max-Q before
  collapses supports a target/replay explanation and raises the priority of the DQN-settings result; no relation weakens it.
- Robustness. Q: does SSP's smoothness buy robustness that tile coding lacks? Pre-declared rule below. Supports or
  removes the "SSP robustness" argument in the write-up; it does not change which cell has the best baseline score.
- Deployment. Q: at matched performance, how do size, quantization loss and latency compare? RUN_003's 30-65x
  parameter gap is an artefact of non-overlapping search ranges, so these numbers are descriptive and a size sweep is
  only worth running if a family's size matters to the conclusion.
- DQN-settings. Q: is the collapse an artefact of the fixed settings? If it disappears for SSP under a variant, the
  RUN_003 tile-vs-SSP gap is partly a settings artefact and the SSP cells should be re-searched under that setting. If no
  effect, that is WEAK evidence (lr/eps were not retuned), so do not conclude the settings are irrelevant.

**Pre-declared interpretation rule (robustness).** SSP's robustness counts only if SSP beats tile coding's retained
score (family score / the same seeds' baseline) by more than bootstrap noise at the same centering and architecture.
Beating discrete is not evidence. Labelling:
- `primary`: tile_coding minus ssp at centering in {none, value}, both architectures, i.e. the 8 cells
  {tile_coding, ssp} x {none, value} x {linear, deep} (user), for every family. Holm-adjusted across all primary
  comparisons of one metric (score and retained corrected as two separate sets).
- `exploratory`: everything else (centering=simple, discrete vs others); raw p-values only. The robustness suite is run
  on all 18 cells (user) but only the primary set supports a claim.
- Unit of analysis = the policy seed (episodes averaged within a seed; bootstrap over seeds, 5000 resamples, unpaired
  across cells). Sensitivity: the same comparisons on seeds whose in-harness baseline mean is >= 475.

**Order of work** (for the eval-run agent): (1) launch policy_pass for the 8 primary cells as the timing pilot; time ONE
cell on a pod before launching all; check `train_summary.json` / `manifest.json` and confirm `policy_verified` for all
seeds. (2) robustness + deployment on those cells (latency alone on a quiet pod), then `analyze_robustness.py`.
(3) the remaining 9 cells, plus `tile_coding/value/deep` once its RUN_003 winner is on R2 (its final eval had not landed
when this was written). (4) DQN-settings variants in parallel pods, after the baseline pass is verified.

### 3.7 Why our SSP reads below the referenced paper, and the search-boundary evidence

The referenced paper (Bartlett et al., "Improving RL with Biologically Motivated Continuous State Representations";
the PDF is the **21st ICCM, 2023** proceedings, which `docs/06-related-work.md` cites as ICCM 2022 -- fix that)
reports CartPole HexSSP terminal reward CI **[496.88, 499.97]** over 10 runs. Its protocol, read from the PDF:

- **Metric:** terminal reward = mean reward over the last 100 episodes of a trial, i.e. the ONLINE training
  return with exploration on. This is our `online_return_mean`, not our headline greedy last-5 mean.
- **Architecture:** HexSSP -> hidden layer of **1024 / 2048 / 4096 rectified-linear neurons** -> actor and critic
  readouts. Rotations in {4,5,6,7}, **scalings = 8**, `length_scale` in **[0.01, 1.0]**, eps in [0.2, 0.6],
  gamma_state 0.99, gamma_action 0.9. Hyperparameters tuned by 100 NNI simulated-annealing experiments.
- **Baselines:** five tabular discretisations and a deep A2C MLP (SB3). **Tile coding was never tested.**
- **Variance claim:** HexSSP sigma^2 = 127.84 vs deep A2C MLP sigma^2 = 20966.52 (Levene p = 0.017).
- **Robustness claim:** RatBox travel speed halved (10,000 -> 5,000 pix/s); HexSSP held, tabular failed.

**On the paper's own metric we reproduce the paper's number**, using the ported A2C and a LINEAR readout with no
neuron layer (20 seeds, seeds 3000-3019):

   bug, and it is untested.

```
DQN deep    phi(s) -> Linear(64) -> ReLU -> Linear(64) -> ReLU -> Linear(2)
DQN linear  phi(s) -> Linear(2)                      (no hidden layer, no activation)
A2C         phi(s) -> value row + action rows        (linear readout; the port has no hidden-layer support)
```

So the deep arm is already an ordinary ReLU MLP; there is no missing activation to add to it.

### 3.8 Why robustness differs: mechanism measurements

Measured on 5 seeds of each primary cell (policies pulled from R2). `flip` = fraction of consecutive greedy steps
whose action changes on a clean rollout; `margin` = mean (best - second-best) Q; `dQ/dx` = |change in
Q(a0)-Q(a1)| per unit state step, on 256 probe states; retention is the action_delay family from 3.3.

| cell | clean flip | flip @1% noise | margin | dQ/dx (inner) | dQ/dx (outer) | delay retention |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tile_coding/none/linear | 0.598 | 0.089 | 8.56 | 35.85 | 32.79 | 0.542 |
| ssp/none/linear | 0.762 | 0.090 | 3.03 | 10.99 | 19.00 | 0.923 |
| tile_coding/value/deep | 0.542 | 0.103 | 10.67 | 50.56 | 39.24 | 0.278 |
| ssp/value/deep | 0.799 | 0.103 | 1.06 | 4.33 | 6.46 | 0.993 |

Two obvious hypotheses are REFUTED:

- *"SSP's policy is smoother, so stale actions hurt less."* SSP changes its action MORE often (0.76-0.80 vs
  0.54-0.60), not less.
- *"SSP has a bigger decision margin."* SSP's margin is much SMALLER (1.1-3.0 vs 8.6-10.7).

What fits instead is a **control-gain / delay-margin trade**. `dQ/dx` orders delay retention monotonically across
all four cells: 50.6 -> 0.278, 35.8 -> 0.542, 11.0 -> 0.923, 4.3 -> 0.993. The entropy-based reading is that a
high-gain controller tuned for precision has little margin against the phase lag a delay injects, while a low-gain
one absorbs it.

The single-impulse test supports the same trade from the other side. Inject ONE wrong action at step 50 (then
continue greedy), 15 episodes per cell:

| cell | clean length | wrong @50 | survives 500 | wrong @150 | survives 500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tile_coding/none/linear | 500.0 | 500.0 | 1.00 | 500.0 | 1.00 |
| ssp/none/linear | 454.4 | 456.5 | 0.60 | 439.5 | 0.53 |
| tile_coding/value/deep | 482.6 | 500.0 | 1.00 | 482.3 | 0.93 |
| ssp/value/deep | 500.0 | 499.4 | 0.93 | 500.0 | 1.00 |

High gain buys recovery from a one-off disturbance and costs delay margin; low gain is the reverse. That is exactly
the classical trade, and it explains the otherwise paradoxical pattern that the *perfect* baseline
(tile_coding/none/linear, 500.0 clean) collapses under delay while an imperfect one (ssp/none/linear, 454.4 clean)
barely notices.

Consistency check on probe states: decision agreement under a 1%-of-sigma perturbation is slightly HIGHER for SSP
(0.9956-1.0000) than tile coding (0.9854-0.9899), despite SSP's smaller margin, because its dQ/dx is 3-8x smaller.

Confidence: MEDIUM. It is four cells, `dQ/dx` is confounded with representation, and the ordering is one
correlation. The clean test is to vary the gain WITHIN one representation and see whether delay retention follows
(section 6, C2).

## 4. Limitations (known before running)

- Final policy only: robustness and deployment describe the end-of-training policy, including seeds that have collapsed;
  no peak-checkpoint result exists.
- Seeds 3000-3019 are one more draw of 20 seeds; the replicate has the same sampling noise as RUN_003 (seed std 3.6-100).
  Winner configs were chosen on seeds 0-499/1000+, so 3000+ is clean of selection.
- `simple` centering is applied naively to an episodic task and every `simple` winner is at the lower beta bound
  (decision-log 17, RUN_003 4.5); `simple` cells are exploratory only.
- Robustness noise scale is policy-independent and mild on theta; the levels 0.01/0.05/0.1 may all saturate. 20 episodes
  per (seed, condition); seed is the unit, so n = 20 per cell.
- Primary comparison is restricted to 8 cells and is unpaired across cells. Discrete baselines (~220-270) make its
  retained ratios hard to interpret.
- Tile coding vs SSP model sizes come from non-overlapping search ranges (RUN_003 4.4); deployment size comparisons are
  descriptive, not a controlled size-for-performance trade-off.
- Latency is implementation- and machine-specific (pure-Python tile/discrete encoders, NumPy SSP).
- DQN-settings test: lr, eps (and the rest of the winner config) were tuned under buffer 5000 / target update 10 and are not
  retuned; only the 8 listed combinations, only 10 seeds, cells none-centering only.
- `tile_coding/value/deep` is not in the first passes if its RUN_003 winner was not on R2; the primary tile-vs-SSP set
  at value/deep is incomplete until it is added.
- Cost/time figures in the original skeleton were estimates; section 2 now carries measured values and 4.1 records the corrections.

  than re-run (the runner is keyed per seed and condition). Decision-log entry 25.
- **Docker Hub anonymous pull limits killed pods repeatedly** (`toomanyrequests`). Six of the first
  eighteen policy-pass pods died that way; retries succeeded. `RUNPOD_REGISTRY_AUTH_ID` in `.env` is now
  wired into both pod-body builders, so adding a RunPod registry credential fixes it permanently; it is
  unset here, so every launch relied on retries.
- **Two ops bugs were found and fixed mid-run**: the launcher emitted a duplicate `--workers` (argparse
  silently kept the last, so pods could run a different worker count than their vCPU count), and the
  supervisor derived "which cells are outstanding" from the pod list. Because a finished pod self-deletes,
  completed cells looked outstanding and were relaunched, producing duplicate pods. Decisions are now made
  from R2 markers, and duplicate removal only deletes pods whose `JOB_ARGS` are byte-identical.
- **The A2C online-metric gate fails on 3 of 9 cells and that is expected**, not a port defect: the
  legacy reference is a maximum over the top-10 trials of a 10-seed mean with unseeded environments.
  See section 3.6 item 3 for the numbers.
- **Still partial at the time of writing:** section 3.5 (3 of 4 DQN-settings cells in flight) and the
  DQN-settings figure. Latency was measured on ONE quiet pod running all 27 cells back-to-back, which is
  more comparable than 27 hosts, but it is a single sample per cell.

## 5. Conclusions

Confidence labels: **high** = replicated on fresh seeds with a pre-declared rule; **medium** = measured but a small
n, a confound, or one protocol; **low** = hypothesis only. Everything is CartPole-v1, DQN and the ported linear A2C.

**Representations**

1. **Tile coding >= SSP on the headline score, under both algorithms (high).** DQN: tile coding wins 5 of 6 matched
   pairs in RUN_003 and the pattern replicates here (3.1). A2C: tile leads at `none` (499.45 vs 470.00), `value`
   (499.70 vs 473.86) and `simple` (483.46 vs 459.92). Discrete is far behind both (DQN 221-266; A2C 132-300).
2. **SSP is not the better representation under A2C either (high).** The legacy claim that SSP >= tile coding under
   A2C is gone: it rested on the online metric and on reference numbers that are selection maxima (3.6, 3.7). This
   matters because that claim was the main evidence that SSP's benefit is algorithm-dependent; it is not.
3. **The size claim is still unresolved (n/a).** The "30-65x smaller" comparison comes from non-overlapping search
   ranges, and we now know BOTH sides were bound-limited: SSP winners press the `length_scale` floor and, in the
   linear cells, the `n_rotates` ceiling; tile-coding winners press the `lr` floor, the `num_tilings` ceiling and,
   in two cells, the `tiles_per_dim` floor (3.7 and below). No size-for-performance claim is supportable yet.
4. **Deployment: quantization is free, compute is implementation-bound (medium).** fp16 retention is 0.96-1.00 for
   all 27 cells and int8 is >= 0.98 except on the weak deep discrete cells (3.4). Analytic op counts favour tile
   coding by 1-3 orders of magnitude (6-96 vs 1253-67939), but MEASURED latency does not: the slowest cell is
   `tile_coding/none/deep` at 384.8 us against `ssp/none/deep` at 227.5 us, because the tile and discrete encoders
   are pure Python while SSP's is NumPy. The size and compute stories are therefore both currently artifacts of the
   search ranges and of the implementation language.

**Algorithms**

5. **The representation ordering is not optimiser-specific (high).** A2C reproduces the DQN order, so the ranking is
   a property of the encodings. A2C is also two orders of magnitude cheaper per run (10-90 s vs 100-800 s) with no
   variance benefit.
6. **The DQN settings do not explain the tile-vs-SSP gap (high).** The hypothesis this run existed to test -- that
   SSP's learn-then-collapse is an artifact of the frozen settings -- is NOT supported: no variant rescues SSP
   (best case +23.8 at target update 100, and 500 is catastrophic everywhere), while the setting is load-bearing
   for the best cell (`tile_coding/none/linear` -174.7 at 100). So the settings are neither suppressing SSP nor a
   neutral axis to relax (3.5).

**Centering**

7. **`value` centering is harmless; `simple` is harmful or neutral on episodic CartPole, but its sign is
   representation-dependent (high).** `value` winners are interior and produce the best A2C cell (tile/value
   499.70). `simple` costs SSP 10.1 and tile coding 16.0 under A2C -- and every DQN `simple` winner pinned beta at
   the lower bound, i.e. the search switched centering off -- yet `discrete/simple` gains **+168.5** over
   `discrete/none` (3.6). So the "policy freezes when the centered TD error vanishes" explanation fails, and the
   surviving reading is variance reduction helping a representation that is otherwise too noisy to learn.

**Robustness**

8. **Robustness splits by perturbation type, not by representation (high for the split, medium for the mechanism).**
   Tile coding wins the changed-physics families (`pole_length` in all four pairs, plus `wide_init` and `cart_mass`
   margins). SSP wins the control-imperfection families decisively: `action_delay` in all four pairs by 153-342
   points of absolute score and Holm-significant in retained score in all four, and `random_action` in three of
   four. The baseline is a statistical tie in three of the four pairs; only `none/linear` has a higher tile
   baseline (+40.1) (3.3).
9. **The mechanism looks like a control-gain trade, not decision smoothness (medium).** SSP flips its action MORE
   and has a SMALLER Q margin than tile coding, refuting both intuitive explanations; what orders the result is
   `dQ/dx`, the policy's feedback gain (4/4 monotone), with the single-impulse test confirming the other side of
   the trade: high gain recovers from a disturbance, low gain tolerates delay (3.8).
10. **This is not the axis the paper's robustness claim was about.** The ICCM claim is changed physics (RatBox
    speed halved), where tile coding is at least as good as SSP here. SSP's advantage is in stale and randomised
    actions -- which is, per the sim-to-real literature below, the more practically damaging axis.

**Scope and the referenced paper**

11. **We reproduce the paper's number under its own metric, without its architecture (medium).** On terminal
    reward (online, last 100 episodes) our ported A2C reaches 493.8-495.8 with a LINEAR readout, against the
    paper's CI [496.88, 499.97], and shows its low-variance signature (variance 275 vs the paper's 128 and its
    deep-A2C baseline's 20966). Our lower headline numbers are the greedy-versus-online metric, not a regression:
    the online metric ranks the representations the other way round because the winners' exploration rates differ
    by 10x (3.7).
12. **We did not replicate the paper's architecture, and no result here should be read as replicating it.** The
    paper uses HexSSP -> 1024-4096 fixed random ReLU neurons -> readout, rotations {4,5,6,7}, 8 scalings,
    `length_scale` down to 0.01, and compares against tabular discretisation and a deep MLP. Our grid uses a linear
    readout (or a learned 2x64 ReLU MLP in the deep arm), 5 scalings, `length_scale` down to 0.1, and includes tile
    coding. The paper's comparison set never included tile coding, so its conclusions are not contradicted by ours;
    ours are simply about a different question.
13. **The linear arm is a fixed-features-plus-linear-readout result, not a deployment-like one.** Modern sim-to-real
    work uses learned MLPs on normalised observations, which is closer to our deep arm -- and neither arm normalises
    observations, which is standard practice in deployed RL and is the same asymmetry as finding 2 of 3.7.

## 6. Next: the re-search/filter plan and the hypothesis tests

Section 6 is carried forward as a standalone action list in
[`docs/handoffs/06-next-search-bounds-and-hypothesis-tests.md`](../docs/handoffs/06-next-search-bounds-and-hypothesis-tests.md);
that file is the one to hand to whoever runs the next phase.

### 6.1 Validity fixes (cheap, do before any new search)

- **Fix `wall_min`** in `train_summary.json` (it currently records one invocation, not the job) and **add
  `winner_trial`** to the `best_params/*_v2.yaml` files, which today resolve only via an R2 pull and silently
  default to trial 0 if that fails.
- **Shift the search bounds rather than widening them.** The evidence is directional, and with a log-uniform
  prior a widened window lowers the sample density everywhere while spending new budget on the side already shown
  to be worse. Shift so the old bound becomes interior, keep the width in log space, and keep the current winner
  inside the new window so the re-search cannot do worse than what exists:

```
parameter                 current            shift to            old winner lands at
SSP length_scale          0.1  - 1.0         0.032 - 0.3         0.10  -> 50%
SSP n_rotates             4    - 20          4     - 32          (integer; extend the top)
SSP lr                    1e-4 - 0.2         3e-5  - 0.06        ~1e-3 -> 80%
tile lr                   1e-4 - 1e-1        3e-5  - 3e-2        1e-4  -> 17%
tile num_tilings          [16, 32]           [16, 32, 64]       32    -> 50%
tile tiles_per_dim        [10, 12, 16]       [10, 12, 16, 24]   16    -> 67%   (weaker evidence)
SSP n_scales              5                  5 or 8             (paper uses 8; changes kernel shape)
inert, do not touch       tile iht_size, eps, and all centering params (interior, no bound pressure)
```

  Tile coding must be shifted alongside SSP. Its winners press `num_tilings` at the ceiling in 4 of 6 cells and
  `lr` at the floor in 2, and `num_tilings=32` beats 16 in **6 of 6** cells on both median and share-solved
  (`none/linear` 480/56% -> 495/75%; `value/deep` 481/56% -> 496/80%). Shifting SSP alone would hand it an opening
  tile coding never got.
- **Test observation normalisation as a factor**, for SSP first and then for tile coding and discrete, so all
  representations see a comparable domain. This is the scaling asymmetry of 3.7 and it is the single cheapest
  change that could plausibly move the SSP floor. `normalize_state` already exists and `policy_io` supports it, so
  DQN needs no new code; the A2C encoder has no such option and needs a small addition.

### 6.2 The re-search/filter plan (conditional on 6.1 moving something)

- **Stage 0 -- diagnostic, ~$2, hours.** Shifted bounds plus normalisation on/off, about 6 `length_scale` values,
  10 seeds, other parameters held at each winner's values. Run it across all SSP cells and the tile-coding cells.
  A clear gain is trustworthy because holding `lr` fixed biases the design AGAINST the changed representation; a
  null result is inconclusive for the same reason.
- **Stage 1 -- joint search + filter + final eval, only if Stage 0 moves something.** Joint search per cell over
  `length_scale`, `lr`, `n_rotates` and `n_scales` (SSP) or `lr`, `num_tilings`, `tiles_per_dim` (tile), about 150
  trials, then the SAME filter (top-20 x 10 seeds) and final eval (20 fresh seeds) as RUN_003 so the result is
  like-for-like. Roughly 6-12 h of search plus ~5 h of filter and final per DQN cell; ~80 pod-hours for the six SSP
  DQN cells and a similar amount if tile coding is shifted too, i.e. about $10-18 and roughly a day of wall time
  with parallel pods. A2C cells are a few hours each (~$2 for the arm).

### 6.3 Hypothesis tests (new knowledge, independent of 6.2)

- **C1 -- delay-aware training (highest practical value).** Randomise (or compensate for) action delay during
  training and re-test the delay family. This is what the sim-to-real literature actually does about latency, and
  it directly asks whether SSP's delay advantage can be obtained by any representation once delay is part of
  training. If it can, the argument shifts from "use SSP" to "train for the delay".
- **C2 -- the gain/delay mechanism (highest novelty).** Vary the control gain WITHIN one representation -- e.g.
  gain-penalised tile coding, or an SSP `length_scale` sweep -- and test whether delay retention tracks `dQ/dx`.
  That would turn 3.8 from a four-point correlation into a mechanism, and it makes a deployment-useful claim:
  measure `dQ/dx` before shipping rather than measuring delay robustness after.
- **C3 -- the paper's random-ReLU arm, A2C only (~$2).** Fixed random ReLU (1024/4096) -> linear readout, reduced
  100-trial search, run for SSP AND tile coding AND a raw-input baseline so the comparison stays fair. Settles
  whether the paper's level needs random features (3.7 says probably not, since we already reach 494-496 without).
- **C4 -- size-matched sweep.** Lower the tile-coding `iht_size` floor below 32768 and the tile `tiles_per_dim`
  floor, and let SSP `n_rotates` go down to 1-2, so the two meet. This is the outstanding question from finding 3.
- **C5 -- then the existing priority list** from handoff 03 section 6: dimension scaling (stacked history k=2/4/8,
  then Acrobot and LunarLander), centering cleanup on a continuing CartPole, hex-vs-random SSP and
  random-Fourier-feature ablations, then mjlab.

### 6.4 Do not do

- **No ReLU between the linear cells' layers.** Those cells have no hidden layer by design; adding one deletes the
  linear/deep ablation (which is itself a result: deep helps SSP and does not help tile coding or discrete). The
  deep arm already applies ReLU between its hidden layers (3.7).
- **No re-run of the 27 cells.** Their question is answered, the data is verified, and the replicate agrees with
  RUN_003 within noise.
- **No search with unshifted bounds**, and no per-representation tuning of the DQN settings (target update is
  load-bearing for tile coding, so tuning it only for SSP would bias the comparison the other way).
- **No SB3 deep-MLP baseline** unless a reviewer asks: its only role is the paper's variance claim, and we can
  report that comparison as unreproduced while reproducing the SSP side of it.

### 6.5 Literature anchors for the sim-to-real framing

Delay and latency are an active sim-to-real problem and the field's fixes are architectural, which is the context
that makes 3.8 and C1/C2 worth pursuing: "RL for Real-Time VLA Policies" (Stanford), "Learning to Act While Waiting"
(Siemens/Berkeley/Microsoft/ETH), "Delay-Aware Diffusion Policy" (FieldAI/Penn), RAPAC-DP, DEFLECT, and the 2026
survey "RL for Control Systems with Time Delays" (arXiv 2602.00399). Randomised state-transition delays for
transfer: Sandha et al., MLSys 2021. Changed physics is treated by domain randomization as standard (the sim-to-real
surveys, arXiv 2009.13303 and 2502.13187), and the ETH/DeepMind study "What Matters for Sim-to-Online RL on Real
Robots" (arXiv 2602.20220) finds ~10^3 randomised environments are necessary for robust transfer, with online
fine-tuning choices (data retention, warm starts, asymmetric actor/critic updates) as the main practical levers.
