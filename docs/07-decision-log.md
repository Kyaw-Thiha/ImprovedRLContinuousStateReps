# Decision Log

Each entry: decision, one-line rationale. Purpose: avoid re-litigating settled questions.

1. **mjlab chosen over Isaac Lab** for the robotics stack — lighter setup (no Omniverse), no 16GB-VRAM-class GPU floor, easier place to hook a custom observation encoder (plain composable functions vs. nested dataclass configs). Isaac Lab kept as a possible later target once methods are validated cheaply.
2. **Value-based centering made mandatory**, not an optional ablation — core to what this research phase is testing, per explicit direction.
3. **Stage 1 (mjlab CartPole as a pipeline sanity check) dropped entirely** — accepted the risk that debugging Stage 2 issues will be harder to isolate (task vs. centering patch vs. representation); mitigated with a lightweight zero-action sanity check folded into the start of Stage 2 instead of a separate stage.
4. **SAC made optional/deferred, not mandatory** — genuinely different algorithm family (off-policy, replay-buffer) worth including for diversity, but not in RSL-RL's maintained branch and real additional engineering + compute cost (see ASAC's ~3000 GPU-hour figure as a scale signal).
5. **`sspspace` reused directly rather than building a new package** — already exists, Nengo-free, maintained by the same lab (Dumont/Furlong, HexSSP's original authors); building a duplicate package wouldn't have added value. A Torch port is still needed for the GPU-batched mjlab path, but it's a small module, not a package, and is tested for parity against `sspspace`, not shipped as a separate library (for now).
6. **PPO-only (not SAC) at Stage 3 (locomotion)** — mjlab's shipped reward/curriculum configs are specifically tuned for PPO; SAC is unproven at this scale in this ecosystem and Stage 3 is the most compute-expensive place to discover instability.
7. **DQN scoped to discrete-action classic CartPole only** — mjlab's robot tasks are continuous-action; discretizing joints to force DQN compatibility would distort the action space and isn't standard practice in this line of work.
8. **Vision/depth manipulation tasks and rough terrain excluded from current scope** — user's stated interest is quadruped/humanoid locomotion specifically for the "advanced" tier, not vision-based tasks; rough terrain deferred as a further stretch.
9. **Legacy Nengo codebase retired, not extended** — Nengo's neuron-population simulation is the actual source of the compute bottleneck (not the HexSSP encoding math itself, which is plain NumPy); removing it entirely, rather than trying to speed it up, is the fix.
10. **Three separate codebases, not one merged repo** — incompatible dependency stacks (Python 3.9/Nengo vs. Python ≥3.10/modern Torch/CUDA) and no natural relationship between DQN-on-CartPole and a MuJoCo/Warp robotics framework. mjlab consumed via its `mjlab.tasks` entry-point mechanism, never forked.
11. **Staged hyperparameter-tuning reduction (3 full searches + 6 locked-hyperparameter centering sweeps) retired in favor of 9 fully independent per-cell searches**, following Raffin's two-part Optuna guide (`06-related-work.md`) almost verbatim: single seed per trial during search (not averaged), `TPESampler`+`MedianPruner` (replacing `HyperbandPruner`) with an explicit 1/3-budget no-pruning warmup, a separate deterministic evaluation env checked in periodically instead of scoring off the noisy training-reward trace, 500 trials/cell, and a post-hoc top-10-trials/10-seeds-each re-evaluation step to filter lucky-seed winners. Reason: locking centering-off hyperparameters for centering-on runs assumes centering doesn't shift the optimum, which the hyperparameter-fairness literature treats as a real source of bias, not a safe shortcut. Final per-cell reported results also move from 5 seeds to ≥20, per Colas et al.'s statistical-power guidance. See `02-experimental-design.md`.
12. **`sync_to_r2.py`'s `cmd_trial` no longer uploads the entire local `best/` directory on every sync** — it now takes an optional `--best-params-file` naming exactly one file, and syncs nothing for best_params when omitted. The old "upload everything" behavior meant every pod on a multi-pod launch re-uploaded every OTHER pod's (mostly stale, image-baked) yaml on its own sync, and whichever pod synced last clobbered every other cell's already-correct result — confirmed recurring 10+ times across two overnight multi-pod runs (2026-09-27/28) before this fix, each requiring a manual boto3 re-upload to correct. Verified live against the production R2 bucket: the pre-fix timestamps on 18 of 19 best_params objects were clustered within one 9-second window (`2026-09-28T07:31:58`–`07:32:07`), direct evidence of a single pod's blind directory-wide re-upload; post-fix, a scoped sync touches only its named file. `run_hparam_search_dqn.py`'s periodic in-search sync omits the flag entirely (a cell's yaml is only ever written once, at completion, so there's nothing to push earlier); only the one final post-`save_best_params()` sync passes it.
13. **Per-trial CSVs (rewards/states/values, written once per Optuna trial) are now also synced to R2** (`--csv-dir`, new in `cmd_trial`) — previously never backed up at all, by either `cmd_trial` or `cmd_phase`; a lost pod meant permanently losing every episode-level curve for that cell's trials (the trial's final score/params still survived independently in the synced DB, but not the within-trial curve). Safe to `upload_dir()` the whole per-cell tree on every sync, unlike `best_params` — these paths were already namespaced per (representation, centering), though architecture was not (`_cell_data_dir` fixes this, suffixing `_deep` the same way `_best_filename` does, so linear/deep pods for the same cell can't collide once mirrored to one R2 prefix). Synced on both the periodic in-search callback (protects mid-search progress, unlike best_params) and the final sync.
14. **Search, re-filter and final eval all use GREEDY evaluation; candidates are ranked by the last 5 greedy checkpoints, not the single last one** (2026-10-04/05) — the old filter scored the noisy epsilon-greedy online return while the search selected on a greedy checkpoint, and one greedy checkpoint of 500 is a snapshot lottery (DQN's greedy policy swings between ~500 and ~0). Greedy eval is mainstream (SB3 `evaluate_policy`, Raffin's `TrialEvalCallback`). The search objective itself (last greedy checkpoint) is unchanged so resumed trials stay comparable. See `docs/handoffs/02-dqn-v4-greedy-refilter-plan.md`.
15. **Re-filter = flat top-20 candidates x 10 fresh seeds (1000+), final eval = 20 fresh seeds (2000+); filter and final eval run chained on the cell's own pod** (2026-10-05) — the pilot showed config sd ~30 vs seed sd ~90 among the top candidates (ICC 0.10 in ssp/simple/deep), so a bigger pool or a 3-seed cut buys nothing; seeds per candidate and the fresh-seed final eval are the levers. Implemented as 4 parallel worker processes per 4-vCPU pod, each finished (candidate, seed) run appended to a JSONL that is pushed to R2 immediately and restored on restart (`scripts/parallel_jobs.py`), so a hung/terminated pod loses at most 4 runs. Results go to NEW R2 locations (`stage0_cartpole/refilter_v2/<study>/`, `best_params/*_v2.yaml`, Optuna `reeval_v2_*` attrs); the old online-metric results are untouched. Winner rule unchanged (best mean, ties -> lower `np.std`). Reported value per cell = the 20-seed final eval mean +/- std with per-seed values and share of seeds >= 475; the filter winner's own mean is optimistic (max of noisy means).
16. **Stale RUNNING trials are marked FAIL and compensated with extra trials** (2026-10-05) — interrupted pods left RUNNING rows that count toward `remaining = target - len(trials)`. At startup the search now fails them and raises `target_n_trials` by the same number, so every cell still ends with 500 FINISHED trials.
17. **Reward centering is applied to episodic CartPole as-is; results are reported as "centering applied naively to an episodic task"** (2026-10-05) — code checked against Naik et al. 2024 (eq. 4 simple, Algorithm 3 value-based): it matches. The paper itself states centering is not applicable to episodic problems (centered reward becomes degenerate) and converts Mujoco to continuing tasks (reset with a large negative reward). CartPole pays exactly +1 every step, so simple centering drives the centered reward to 0 and removes the failure signal (dose-dependent harm, reproduced on a good ssp/none/deep config). All 18 cells are still run so the grid is complete; interpret the `simple`/`value` cells with this caveat. A termination-penalty (continuing) CartPole variant is a possible later extension, not done. **Addendum (2026-10-06, after all 18 cells finished):** the harm is DQN-specific as far as we know. All six `simple` winners pin beta at the lower search bound (1.3e-5..9.2e-5), i.e. the search switches centering off; `simple` finals were SSP 311.5 (linear) / 363.6 (deep) vs tile 496.3 / 479.5. `value` centering was harmless (tile 495.4 linear / 498.0 deep; SSP 468.7 / 474.6). Under legacy A2C (different algorithm and an online metric, NOT comparable) `simple` looked benign (SSP 488.6, tile 490.9, discrete 378 > none 118). Hypothesis, untested: in A2C the policy is a separate parameter set that freezes when the centered TD error goes to zero, while in DQN the greedy policy is the argmax of values that decay toward zero. So do not read this entry as 'centering hurts on episodic CartPole' for all algorithms until A2C is re-evaluated under the greedy protocol. Full 18-cell table: `runs/RUN_003.md`.
   **UPDATE (2026-10-06, RUN_004): the A2C caveat this entry asked for is now measured, and it cuts both ways.**
   Under the new greedy A2C protocol `simple` centering is NOT benign and its sign depends on the representation:
   `ssp/simple` 459.92 vs `ssp/none` 470.00 (-10.1), `tile_coding/simple` 483.46 vs 499.45 (-16.0), but
   `discrete/simple` 300.46 vs `discrete/none` 131.95 (+168.5). The legacy "benign under A2C" reading came from
   the old online metric and a reference number that was a selection maximum, so it should not be used. The
   hypothesis that A2C is protected because a separate policy freezes when the centered TD error vanishes is
   therefore NOT supported; the surviving explanation is the weaker one, that centering acts as variance
   reduction and so helps a representation that is otherwise too noisy to learn (discrete), while costing a
   little where the representation already works. The DQN-specific part of this entry stands. See RUN_004
   sections 3.3 and 3.6.

18. **Filter/final-eval initial weights follow the run seed; the search keeps the legacy fixed init** (2026-10-05) — `DQN.__init__` used `torch.manual_seed(0)`, so `seed` never changed weight init. `init_seed=0` keeps the legacy behavior (search unchanged); filter and final eval use `init_seed=-1` (weights follow the seed). The init experiment (trials 154, 285) showed no significant effect on mean or spread.
19. **eval_v3 saves the FINAL policy only (no peak or intermediate checkpoints)** (2026-10-06) — decided with the user. The question eval_v3 asks is how robust/small/fast the policy a practitioner would actually ship is, and that is the policy at the end of training; picking each seed's best checkpoint would select on the same noisy 10-episode greedy evaluations that entries 14-15 showed to be a lottery (DQN's greedy policy swings between ~500 and ~0), and would make robustness numbers optimistic in exactly the cells that collapse (SSP, discrete). The learn-then-collapse behaviour is therefore part of what is measured, and the seed-level stability numbers come from the 15 greedy checkpoints kept in `train_runs.jsonl`. Saved by `experiments/trial_cartpole_dqn.py` (`save_policy_path`) via `rl/policy_io.py` after ALL evaluation (tile-coding IHT allocation is order-dependent). A policy counts as done only when the in-memory verify replay matches the reloaded file exactly (`max_abs_diff` 0.0); `scripts/policy_pass_worker.py` re-verifies from the saved file before uploading. Layout: `scripts/eval_v3_layout.py`; nothing in `refilter_v2/`, `best_params/`, `optuna_dbs/` is ever written by eval_v3 code.
20. **eval_v3 policy pass: 20 seeds per cell on seeds 3000-3019, env seeded** (2026-10-06) — decided with the user. Seeds 3000+ are disjoint from the search (0-499), filter (1000+) and final eval (2000+), so the pass is an independent replicate of the RUN_003 final eval and can be compared against it directly (a check that the 20-seed numbers are reproducible, and an honest second draw for the winner's curse). Unlike earlier phases (entry 15; RUN_003 caveat "environment resets are unseeded"), the trial is run with `seed_env=True`: the first training reset is seeded with the run seed, the first greedy-checkpoint evaluation with seed+1,000,000 and the verify replay with seed+2,000,000, so a run is reproducible given its seed and the code. Initial weights follow the seed (`init_seed=-1`, as in entry 18). The winner config is read from `best_params/*_v2.yaml` (read only); `tile_coding/value/deep` joins the pass when its winner is on R2 (its final eval had not landed when this was written).
21. **Robustness observation noise is policy-independent and MILD on the pole angle; inspect the pilot cell before adding levels** (2026-10-06) — `obs_noise_f` adds N(0, (f*sigma)^2) to the observation the agent sees, with sigma a single fixed vector (`noise_sigma` in `_shared/probe_states.npz`, from `scripts/make_probe_states.py`) = per-dimension std pooled over 50 seeded episodes of a scripted PD balancing controller run with 10% random actions: [0.4341, 0.3061, 0.0272, 0.2106]. It is policy-independent on purpose (same noise for every policy and cell, so common random numbers hold) and a pooled-over-controller scale is used because a clean controller balances so tightly (theta std ~0.008) that sigma-scaled noise would be meaningless. Consequence: the levels 0.01/0.05/0.1 are small on theta (0.1 -> about 0.0027 rad), so those conditions may saturate at 500 for every policy and then discriminate nothing. Decision rule: run the pilot cell first, look at the `obs_noise_*` rows, and only then decide whether to add higher levels (0.25, 0.5) to `rl/perturbations.py`; do not add levels before looking, and do not describe saturated levels as robustness evidence.
22. **Random-action and action-delay semantics in the robustness suite** (2026-10-06) — `random_action_p`: each step, with probability p the applied action is replaced by a UNIFORM draw over the action set, which may coincide with the policy's decision, so with 2 actions the wrong-action rate is p/2 (`random_action_0.05` means 2.5% wrong actions). `action_delay_d`: the action applied at step t is the one decided at step t-d; CartPole has no no-op, so the first d applied actions are filled with the policy's decision on the RESET observation. Random replacement is applied after the delay. Every robustness episode runs on `policy.clone()` so tile-coding IHT growth never leaks between episodes or conditions, and episode seeds depend only on (condition, episode index) (common random numbers across policies and cells). Suite: 21 conditions in 10 families, 20 episodes per (seed, condition).
23. **Robustness: run on all 18 cells, but the pre-declared SSP-vs-tile claim is made only on 8 cells, with Holm correction** (2026-10-06) — decided with the user. The suite is run on every cell for completeness (exploratory), but the primary claim is restricted to {tile_coding, ssp} x {none, value} x {linear, deep}: the `simple` cells are mostly centering switched off by the search (every `simple` winner sat at the lower beta bound; entries 17 and RUN_003 4.5) and the discrete cells have baselines of ~220-270, so retained-score ratios there are not interpretable. `scripts/analyze_robustness.py` labels tile-minus-SSP at the same centering and architecture `primary` and everything else `exploratory` (raw p-values only); primary p-values are Holm-adjusted per metric (score and retained as two separate sets). Interpretation rule: SSP's robustness counts as a finding only if SSP beats tile coding's retained score at the same centering/architecture by more than bootstrap noise; beating discrete is not evidence. The unit is the policy seed (episodes averaged within a seed, bootstrap over seeds, unpaired across cells), and a sensitivity pass repeats everything on seeds whose in-harness baseline mean is >= 475 so retention is not dominated by failed baselines.
24. **DQN-settings test: replay buffer and target update, 10 paired seeds, lr/eps NOT retuned** (2026-10-06) — decided with the user. Question: is SSP's learn-then-collapse (RUN_003 4.3) an artefact of the never-tuned fixed DQN settings (buffer 5000, target update every 10 updates)? Variants (`scripts/run_policy_pass.py --variant-buffer-size / --variant-target-update-freq`): buffer 5000 vs 50000, target update 10 vs 100 vs 500 (names like `buf50000_tu100`); cells {tile_coding, ssp} x none x {linear, deep}; 10 seeds 3000-3009, paired with the baseline pass seeds (same initial weights and env seed stream); no policies saved (`dqn_settings/<study>/<variant>/`). Runs right after the baseline policy pass is verified and before any size sweep. Caveat that governs interpretation: the winners' lr and eps were tuned under the baseline settings and are NOT re-tuned per variant, so "no effect" is weak evidence (a retune might have found one) while "the collapse disappears" is informative (it shows the collapse depends on those settings at the tuned lr/eps).
25. **Observation-noise levels extended to 0.25/0.5/1.0 after the robustness pilot** (2026-10-06, during RUN_004) — decided during the run, as RUN_004 section 1 requires ("look at the pilot cell first, then decide whether to add 0.25 and 0.5"). `obs_noise_f` adds N(0, (f * noise_sigma)^2) with the policy-independent pooled scale [0.4341, 0.3061, 0.0272, 0.2106], so at the pre-registered maximum 0.1 the pole-angle noise is 0.0027 rad against a 0.209 rad termination limit (~1% of it). The first two pilot cells (discrete/none/linear baseline 257.5; tile_coding/simple/deep baseline 453.2; 20 seeds x 20 episodes) retained 0.989-1.028 and 0.998-1.009 on the three levels — indistinguishable from baseline, with one level scoring ABOVE baseline. The suite itself is not broken: the same runs degraded hard on other families (action_delay_1 retained 0.29-0.66, action_delay_2 0.09-0.35, pole_length_x0.5 0.65-0.85). So the noise axis was simply not being probed, and the three levels were added (at 1.0 the noise equals the pooled state scale in every dimension, and 13% of the termination angle on theta). Consequence: a cell now has 24 conditions, i.e. 480 records; robustness jobs are keyed per (policy seed, condition) and resumable, so the cells that had already finished under the 21-condition set only recompute the three new levels. `rl/perturbations.FAMILIES` is the single source of truth and `tests/test_perturbations.py` derives the expected count from it, so the extension cannot silently desynchronise the suite from its expectations.

26. **The representation study moves off CartPole; CartPole becomes the negative control** (2026-10-06) — decided with the user. CartPole is 4 unstructured scalars, bounded, grid-cheap, and cliff-edged at termination: the regime where a local basis is expected to win, and where SSP's structural properties (relative/unbounded/periodic state, composition) have nothing to attach to. The next representation tests go to continuing MuJoCo tasks whose state has that structure — Reacher with a resampled target, Swimmer with raw wrapped angles, Pusher — with an invariance/shift evaluation, a matched tile-coding arm, a learned-network arm, and a periodic-state encoder arm. mjlab Go1/G1 locomotion is deferred (proprioceptive state has no translation invariance; the most expensive place to test). Rationale and citations: `08-ssp-vs-tile-coding-theory-and-hypotheses.md`, `09-ssp-centering-task-survey.md`; task table in `05-tasks-and-benchmarks.md`.
27. **Centering on episodic tasks is re-tested, not reported as a limitation** (2026-10-06) — updates entry 17. De Asis, Elsayed & He (arXiv:2605.04368) prove a termination-corrected generalization of differential TD that preserves policy ordering, extending reward centering to episodic problems. Our "simple centering is harmful on episodic CartPole" result may be a property of the naive formulation rather than of episodic centering, so CartPole is re-run under the corrected formulation instead of carrying a caveat.
28. **The legacy A2C winner yamls have no traceable provenance; the A2C arm is unverified until this is fixed** (2026-10-06) — the nine `outputs/hparam_search/best/*.yaml` files the A2C arm replayed match no trial in either surviving A2C Optuna DB, no per-trial artefact under `outputs/hparam_search`, and no paper-era record in `origin/main`; the legacy A2C trial has no seed handling at all (`grep -c seed` = 0 in `experiments/trial_cartpole_nengo.py`), so its "10 re-evaluation seeds" were unseeded re-runs and the reported value is a max over the top-10 candidates. Evidence: `08-...md` section 7. No claim of the form "SSP is worse under A2C than reported" until winners are re-derived from the DBs.
29. **No legacy recreation and no hyperparameter re-search** (2026-10-06) — recreation of the old code is not what the field requires (Eimer et al., ICML 2023: if a baseline's tuning information is unavailable, re-tune rather than reuse; Dodge et al., EMNLP 2019: claims are budget-conditional, and a `max`-of-N number is not a measurement) and it is expensive. A reduced re-search is also not required: the comparison protocol already satisfies the standard (identical budget and search-space treatment per arm; disjoint tuning and test seeds). What is required is the reporting (search space, budget, selection criterion, seed split, both greedy and online metrics) plus a one-axis sensitivity statement where a bound was pressed.

30. **Compute gating for the SSP question: free reanalyses and adversarial best-shot tuning first; no re-search** (2026-10-06) — decided with the user. A comparison against an under-tuned arm is invalid (Eimer et al.; Dodge et al.), so SSP must get its best shot — but the form of that shot is targeted falsification attempts, not another 500-trial search, because the CartPole metric is ceiling-limited and the bound-pressure evidence is directional. Order of work: (1) **free reanalyses of existing artefacts** (no training): tail/threshold/CVaR statistics over the 27 cells x 24 conditions x 20 seeds of `robustness_runs.jsonl`, and the interference measure on the saved policies; (2) **targeted SSP best-shot attempts**, each a small sweep at 10 seeds: observation normalisation (the measured, never-tested domain asymmetry), `length_scale` below the old 0.1 floor (the referenced paper's floor is 0.01), `n_rotates` above 20, and the SSP rotation draw (encoder-draw variance); (3) **C1/C2** to test whether the delay advantage is substitutable by training (delay-randomised training; gain-penalised tile coding); (4) MuJoCo scale work is **gated** on (2) or (3) moving something. Not funded: the shifted-bound 500-trial re-search, the legacy A2C recreation, mjlab.

31. **If SSP is to be the protagonist, the paper is "structured spatial encodings for learned policies" — not SSP vs tile coding, and not locomotion** (2026-10-06) — decided with the user. The evidence we hold does not support a real-world advantage for SSP as a state encoder versus a local basis: the headline score is parity or a small loss, the size story is a memory-for-compute trade that loses on the currency deployment cares about, and the one large effect (delay tolerance) is confounded with the gains the search chose for each representation. The comparison itself was also against the wrong family: no deployed system uses tile coding as a policy encoder; they use learned networks on relative spatial observations, usually with positional/Fourier or set-based encodings. So the only version in which SSP is the protagonist, is real-world relevant, and is fundable is: SSP(-style) spatial encodings as the *input representation to a learned policy* on relative-spatial observation tasks, with **learned MLP / normalised MLP / random-Fourier-feature / set-based (Deep Sets-style) baselines**, evaluated on sample efficiency, generalisation to unseen relative configurations, delay/noise robustness and tail metrics. This is the docs' original Stage-2 idea (HexSSP on `ee_to_cube`/`cube_to_goal`) with the correct baselines and a task class that is cheap enough to run without a per-representation search (published configs + one shared tuning budget). If that claim is not motivating either, the SSP thread stops here: CartPole becomes a negative-control section of the centering work, and no further SSP compute is spent. RatBox itself remains out of scope (not interesting to the user); the arm/reaching task is its real-world analogue.

32. **SSP as protagonist = rank 1 (content-addressable memory with cue completion); rank 2 (variable slots) folded in as a task axis** (2026-10-06) — decided with the user. Rank 1 is the only problem that is simultaneously real-world relevant (POMDP memory, occlusion, sensor dropout, rare-event recall, bounded on-device budgets) and literally what the architecture was designed around: the SPA's declarative-memory module, where recall happens from a partial or noisy cue. Rank 2 (a variable number of slots held and queried) is the SPA's working-memory pattern, but it is *not* made a separate thesis because the variable-slot problem is already owned by the object-centric line (MetaSlot arXiv:2505.20772 attacks the fixed-slot limit directly; plus Slot-MPC 2605.14937, COMET 2606.14418, FOCI 2609.08743, World-as-Graph 2609.38927, GNN relational RL 2009.12462). Benchmarks: **POPGym** diagnostic subset (Concentration, Count Recall, Repeat Previous, Autoencode) as the cheap filter with 13 published baselines, then **MIKASA-Robo** (ShellGame for rank 1; BunchOfColors / SeqOfColors / ChainOfColors for rank 2) for robotics credibility. Simulators: POPGym needs none; MIKASA uses ManiSkill3 (GPU-parallel, not MuJoCo); MuJoCo-with-observation-masking is the acceptable middle option; avoid RLBench (no efficient parallelisation, per the MIKASA authors) and navigation tasks (POPGym's memoryless MLP beat every memory model on Labyrinth). Plan and kill points in `10-...md` section 13.4: stage 0 encoder-only T0-T7 (minutes) -> stage 1 POPGym (~96 GPU-h) -> stage 2 MIKASA -> stage 3 deployment axis. Always report mean-of-last-k under a fixed budget rather than POPGym's MMER (max-over-epochs, selection-inflated), and always include PosMLP and GRU baselines.
33. **POPGym is a filter, not a headline; the claim must be capability + footprint, not accuracy** (2026-10-06) — judgment recorded so it is not rediscovered. POPGym's best general-purpose memory model is a plain **GRU**; FART, FWP, S4D, DNC, LMU, TCN and IndRNN all underperform it in RL, the authors report tasks where all 13 models plateau together (Autoencode, Battleship) and navigation tasks where the *memoryless* MLP wins. Two readings, both hostile to an accuracy-only pitch: either the tasks sit within an RNN's reach, or architectural memory innovation has low leverage in RL (POPGym suspects PPO is the bottleneck). Consequence: a VSA memory that beats a GRU by a few points on POPGym is a footnote. The defensible claims are (a) a **capability** a GRU does not have — content-addressable recall from a corrupted cue, algebraic query, invertible transitions — and (b) a **footprint** advantage — O(1) writes, bounded memory, integer arithmetic — which POPGym's per-model efficiency tables make comparable against published numbers. Any POPGym result must be paired with a robotics task (MIKASA) to be read at all. Evidence the field still cares, in the direction that matters: the memory-benchmark line is actively growing and moving to robotics in 2025-2026 (MIKASA ICLR 2026 with 32 robot tasks plus online-RL/offline-RL/VLA baselines; RoboMME arXiv:2603.04639; MemoryBench, Fang et al. 2025, concurrent; a memory-improvable-domains suite arXiv:2508.00046). Robotics is where the current attention is.
