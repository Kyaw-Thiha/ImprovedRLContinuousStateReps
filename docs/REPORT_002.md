# Report 002: reward centering x SSP encoding on a continuing Go1 task

Written 2026-11-01, revised the same day after review (decision 70). Supersedes the mjlab-track open items of REPORT_001;
the POPGym and supervised results there are unchanged. Every number comes from a run file in
`mjlab_centering/results/merged_r2` (rebuilt from the per-run R2 mirrors by `analysis/restore_runs.py`) unless marked as a
measurement taken outside a training run. Figures: `docs/mjlab_centering/figures/report/01..10_*.png`, generated by
`mjlab_centering/analysis/make_report_figs.py`.

## 1. What was run

Task: Go1 velocity tracking in mjlab, made continuing (no time-outs, no terminal flags, a fall costs 10, a +1 per-step
reward offset). **That is the legacy protocol**: after the G1 diagnosis (section 4.1) the default became "keep the
time-outs, a fall is not terminal" (decision 77), so every number below is labelled legacy and new cells use the new
wrapper. PPO (rsl-rl 5.5.1) with mjlab's tuned Go1 defaults; 2048 envs; 800 iterations unless stated.
Metrics: `J_task` = mean per-step task reward of the deterministic policy (max about 0.12, trained about 0.10, a standing
policy 0.077); `tracking` = linear-velocity term normalised to 0-1 (standing 0.28, trained 0.89).

1. Gate: continuing wrapper vs mjlab's episodic env (Go1, 2 seeds). Fig 01.
2. Centering step size eta and r_bar initialisation (search seeds 1000-1002). Fig 02.
3. SSP length scale (search seeds). **Uninformative, see 4.2.**
4. Equal-width control: raw vs random-Fourier vs HexSSP at 1068 features (3 seeds). Fig 03.
5. Main factorial: centering x {raw, SSP} x gamma, 10 paired claim seeds (2000-2009). Figs 04, 05.
6. Critic-fix rivals: value clipping off, return normalisation (10 seeds). Fig 06.
7. Shock block: +4 reward offset at iteration 600, branched from checkpoints (10 seeds). Fig 07.
8. Linear-critic slice: NumPy TD(0) and actor-critic on continuing Catch with exact values. Figs 08, 09.
9. G1 reference probe. Fig 10.

## 2. Results that stand

1. **Gate passes on substance.** Wrapped and episodic Go1 are indistinguishable (tracking 0.89 vs 0.89, J_task 0.103 vs
   0.103; standing 0.28 / 0.077), so the wrapper, the fall penalty and the metric do not change learning. It missed one
   pre-set ratio criterion by 0.4% (a badly chosen relative threshold) and was accepted on substance, logged as a deviation.
2. **Centering is the main effect.** gamma 0.999, 10 seeds: J_task 0.0296 -> 0.0850 (+0.0554, CI [+0.0507, +0.0603]) and
   tracking 0.31 -> 0.71. At gamma 0.99 (3 raw seeds): +0.0375 [+0.0338, +0.0395]. Every uncentered arm fails to learn to walk
   (tracking 0.30-0.31 in 30 of 30 runs) and drifts to J_task about 0.03, *below* the standing policy; their action std
   grows from 1.0 to about 1.3. Centered raw: 8 of 10 seeds leave the standing floor (tracking 0.66-0.87), 2 do not (0.31, 0.32).
3. **Centering is not interchangeable with cheaper critic fixes.** Plain 0.0296 / tracking 0.306; value clipping off 0.0679 /
   0.308; return normalisation 0.0236 / 0.299; centering 0.0850 / 0.710 (n=10 each). Clipping off recovers most of the reward
   gap and none of the behaviour gap.
4. **eta, not the fixed point, is what centering gets wrong first.** eta 0.01 fails (0.057 / 0.317); eta 0.03 with zero init
   collapses on 1 of 3 seeds (mean 0.071); eta 0.1 / 0.3 work (0.088 / 0.092); eta 0.03 with r_bar initialised from the first
   batch gives 0.091 on all three seeds. eta and the initialisation were chosen together and are one intervention.
5. **At equal width, HexSSP beats a random code.** 1068 features, centered, gamma .99, 3 seeds: raw 0.097 / 0.83, HexSSP
   0.082 / 0.54, random Fourier 0.072 / 0.44. A 22x wider input costs about a quarter of the reward by itself; HexSSP recovers
   a bit more than half of it (+0.010 J_task, +0.10 tracking vs random Fourier) and does not reach raw input.
6. **Linear slice.** Without centering, an offset destroys value learning for features that cannot express a constant (raw:
   state-dependent error 147 at gamma .999 vs 0.03 centered; tile 27.9 vs 0.007). A bias unit removes the problem for the
   *value function* (0.033) but not for the *controller*: uncentered raw+bias control collapses at every gamma (-0.067 per
   step) and centered control recovers to only 0.010; tile coding with centering reaches 0.106. SSP was not special in this
   slice: tile + centering was the best feature set. The 220-vs-1.2 "centering is worse with a bias" reading in an earlier draft
   was a constant k/(1-gamma) offset, 98.6% of the number, and cannot change an action (decision 54).

## 2b. Protocol revision and the re-confirmation under it (2026-11-02)

The 800-iteration factorial above was run under the legacy wrapper (no time-outs at all). Two things then changed the
protocol. First, the G1 failure was traced to a bug of ours: removing the fall termination also removed the reset that
termination triggers, so a fallen robot lay on the floor until the next time-out and could not learn (decision 81;
verified by driving a G1 to a fall and watching the episode counter and root height). Second, the corrected default now
keeps both terminations and hides only the fall from PPO: `fell_over` stays, so the env resets the robot in place;
`time_out` stays, so the segment is truncated and bootstrapped; `dones` comes from the truncation only. That is the
Wan-style "continuing with predefined resets" this study intended, and it is what every new cell uses (decision 77,
81).

A pilot at 2000 iterations on the corrected protocol reproduces the main effect and moves the interpretation (decision
89, campaign `fix1-b7c40a`, 2 seeds per cell):

| Go1, gamma .999, 2000 its | track_lin | J_task | walks |
|---|---|---|---|
| offset 0, no centering | 0.309 | 0.0710 | 0/2 |
| offset 0, value centering | 0.885 | 0.0998 | 2/2 |
| offset 1.0, no centering | 0.303 | 0.0384 | 0/2 |
| offset 1.0, value centering | 0.880 | 0.0994 | 2/2 |

Paired dJ +0.0288 (offset 0) and +0.0611 (offset 1.0); dtrack +0.576 and +0.577, at two seeds per cell.

**The offset-0 cells in the table above are misleading and are superseded.** Ten fresh seeds (3000-3009) of the same
cell give uncentered 10/10 walking (track 0.882, J 0.0993) against centered 10/10 (0.884, 0.1000), paired dJ +0.0007
(se 0.0003) - no effect at all; at gamma .99 it is also a tie (0.888 vs 0.890, 3 seeds). Both arms show the time-outs
firing and falls at 0.02 per 1k, so this is not a protocol difference. The pilot's uncentered offset-0 reading of 0.309
was seed noise (decision 94). The supported statement is the opposite of the earlier draft: with the +1 offset,
uncentered Go1 fails on 20 of 20 seeds while centered walks on 19 of 20; without the offset, both arms walk. Centering is
necessary when a large constant reward offset is present. Whether the high gamma is also required is **not** isolated: the
uncentered failure appears at gamma .999 on Go1 (20 seeds), at gamma .99 on G1 (6 seeds) and, under the legacy protocol, at
gamma .99 on Go1 (3 seeds), so the evidence points at the offset and does not show a gamma dependence.

**G1 under the same protocol** (gamma .99, offset 1, **6 seeds per arm**, penalty 5.7 - the per-robot rule of decision
82, because Go1's penalty of 10 is ~230x G1's per-step reward and costs it most of the tracking reward): uncentered
**0 of 6** seeds walk (mean track 0.312, J 0.0584) against centered **6 of 6** (mean 0.793, J 0.0883), paired
**dJ +0.0299 (se 0.0012)**, dtrack +0.481. The centering main effect therefore reproduces on a humanoid. At gamma .999
with no offset the humanoid is unstable at this budget and centering does not help - uncentered 0.527 (2 of 3 walk)
against centered 0.398 (0 of 3), paired dJ -0.0036 - so no claim is made at that cell.

The 20-seed confirmation at offset 1.0 is now in (campaign `night1-3e71b4`, fresh claim seeds 3000-3019, 2000
iterations, corrected wrapper, 2048 envs):

| Go1, gamma .999, offset 1.0 | n | track_lin | J_task | walks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no centering | 20 | 0.307 | 0.0459 | **0/20** (max 0.328) |
| value centering | 20 | 0.854 | 0.0985 | **19/20** (one seed stays at the floor) |

Paired, all 20 seeds: **dJ = +0.0526, se 0.0019**, dtrack +0.547. Every paired difference is positive and they span
+0.0374 to +0.0708. The uncentered arm does not merely underperform: it never leaves the standing floor on any of
twenty seeds, while nineteen of the twenty centered seeds land in a 0.016-wide band around 0.88.

## 3. Results that are provisional

1. **SSP x centering interaction: -0.0220 [-0.0276, -0.0169] at gamma .999 (-0.0180 at .99).** Centering buys about 40% less
   (+0.0334 vs +0.0554) when the input is the 1068-wide SSP code. SSP alone is neutral (+0.0051 [+0.0008, +0.0094]).
   **Provisional because 800 iterations did not allow convergence for the centered arms** (section 4.4) and SSP was never tuned
   (4.2). The supported statement is "at this budget and an untuned encoder, SSP lags raw under centering", not "SSP and
   centering interact".
2. **Interaction precision.** CI half-width 0.0054 against a 0.0025 margin (3% of the best cell): no tight equivalence claim in
   either direction.

## 4. What was wrong, and what was withdrawn

1. **G1 does not walk because the wrapper removed the time-outs.** The earlier G1 probe ran at offset 1.0 without
   centering, i.e. the cell that also fails on Go1, so it never tested the conversion. Diagnosing it properly (campaign
   `g1diag-7c41e2`, single seed 1000, 2000 iterations, gamma .99, decision 73-75; last-5-eval means, standing
   `track_lin_frac` 0.267):

   | wrapper | time-outs | falls terminal | centering | track_lin | J_task | walks |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | continuing (as used in waves 1-9) | no | no | none | 0.307 | 0.0472 | no |
   | continuing | no | no | value | 0.309 | 0.0458 | no |
   | mjlab episodic | yes | yes | none | 0.813 | 0.0926 | **yes** |
   | time-outs kept, falls non-terminal | yes | no | none | 0.811 | 0.0924 | **yes** |

   Three consequences: the fall termination is not needed (time-outs kept with non-terminal falls walks as well as the
   fully episodic env), centering does not compensate for the missing resets, and Go1 walks at the same no-time-out cell,
   so the Go1-only wrapped = episodic gate did not generalise to the humanoid. The mechanism shows in the rows: the
   no-time-out arms collapse the action std to 0.55-0.57 with zero falls and never leave standing, while both reset arms
   hold it near 0.38 and take off after iteration ~1100. Version pins are unlikely: the G1 task config and robot XML are
   identical between mjlab 1.3.0 (the third-party walker) and our pinned 1.6.0. Single seed each, so these are
   diagnoses. Full record: decisions 73-76, `mjlab_centering/runs/RUN_M04_g1_diagnosis.md`.
2. **The SSP length-scale sweep was uninformative.** It ran at gamma .99 with no centering at offset c, where every arm sits at
   the floor, so "length scale makes no difference" (0.056 / 0.057 / 0.058) carries no information. SSP was never tuned: the 8x8
   hexagonal grid, length scale 0.3, the KL-adaptive learning-rate schedule and the network size were all left at one setting.
3. **The shock block cannot test its pre-registered claim.** See section 5.
4. **800 iterations did not allow convergence.** The length was set from the plateau of a no-offset reference arm (about 600);
   centered offset arms start improving at about iteration 400. Slope of the eval metric over the last 250 iterations, per 100
   iterations, mean over seeds with 95% bootstrap CI: raw centered tracking +0.042 [+0.017, +0.074] (10/10 seeds rising),
   J_task +0.0033 [+0.0019, +0.0047]; SSP centered tracking +0.015 [+0.007, +0.024] (9/10 rising), J_task -0.0001 (4/10);
   raw uncentered tracking -0.008 (2/10 rising). So the centering main effect survives (uncentered arms are flat or falling for
   about 500 iterations), but whether SSP-centered catches raw-centered is unknown.
5. **"Stuck at the standing floor" applies to tracking, not to J_task** (item 2 of section 2).
6. Earlier corrections kept for the record: "SSP does not train" was a width artefact (decision 65); the shock baseline taken
   from a branch with no pre-shock evals (62); duplicate writers interleaving one JSONL (62); the 95%-of-baseline recovery
   metric (68).

## 5. The shock block, honestly

Design: at iteration 600 a +4 per-step offset is added (per-step task reward is about 0.1) and training continues from the
mother's checkpoint for 200 more iterations; a resumed-but-unshocked control runs from the same checkpoint.

- **Branching is validated.** 20 resumed-unshocked controls vs their unbranched mothers: mean +0.0012, 95% CI [-0.0020,
  +0.0045], between-seed SD 0.031.
- **A centered arm is not immune.** J_task falls from 0.082 to a minimum of 0.049 at iteration 700 and is 0.057 at 800, against
  0.088 for its unshocked control: not recovered inside the 200-iteration window.
- **The uncentered comparison is withdrawn.** The uncentered shocked arm falls monotonically to about 0.001 by iteration 800
  and never recovers. The earlier "recovers half its dip in 79 vs 64 iterations" is an artefact: the half-dip level is derived
  from the minimum, which occurs at the end, so the first post-shock evaluation already counts as recovered. And the uncentered
  arm had never learned (J_task already below standing), so there is nothing meaningful to shock.
- **Pre-registered P5 (centered recovers faster than uncentered) is inconclusive.** A meaningful version shocks a working
  policy trained without the offset, uses a moderate shift, and allows a window of at least 400 iterations.

## 6. What we can conclude

1. Reward centering is a large, real effect on this continuing GPU-parallel robot task, and it is not a restatement of "fix
   the critic". The size of the effect on *behaviour* (tracking 0.31 -> 0.71) is much larger than the J_task difference suggests.
2. Centering works through convergence speed of the reward-rate estimate (eta and the initialisation decide success), and it
   is unreliable per seed at this budget (8 of 10).
3. SSP is neutral as an encoder replacement, beats a random code of the same width, and has not been shown to help or to hurt
   under centering once width and tuning are controlled.
4. Everything about SSP x centering needs longer runs and an actual tuning pass (plan: `docs/mjlab_centering/03-ssp-plan.md`).
5. G1 and the shock comparison are open for specific, cheap, named reasons, not closed.

## 7. Limits

One robot, one task, one budget (800 iterations, not converged for centered arms), 10 seeds per cell (the 20-seed top-up was
not run), gamma .99 raw cells have 3 seeds (cut for budget), the action-rate shock arm was cut before running, eta and the
r_bar initialisation are confounded, the equal-width control has 3 seeds and one length scale, G1 is not in the factorial, and
its diagnosis (section 4.1) is one seed per arm.
